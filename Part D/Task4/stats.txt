Task 4 -- Channel count doubled (1 -> 2)
==========================================
(Same trace, same scheduler (FRFCFS), same DRAM/timing, same address
mapping, same row policy -- ONLY the channel count changed relative to
Task 1. RoBaRaCoCh places Channel at the least-significant field, so
channel selection comes from the lowest address bit above the cache-line
offset.)

--- Raw per-channel stats reported by Ramulator2 ---
memory_system_cycles (global)  : 8,641,291

                          Channel 0        Channel 1
row_hits                  523,908          917,069
row_misses                 7,131           14,861
row_conflicts             189,920          589,104
read_latency (sum)        148,805,726      260,244,657
true reads (hits+miss+conf) 262,211        262,209

--- Channel load balance (unexpected finding) ---
Total row-buffer events (hits+misses+conflicts): Ch0 = 720,959 (32.2%),
Ch1 = 1,521,034 (67.8%). The two channels are NOT evenly loaded (roughly
1:2.1) despite Channel sitting at the least-significant address bit (which
should, in principle, split traffic evenly). This is a real, measured
imbalance in this run and is worth noting: Channel 1 is the effective
bottleneck channel, carrying more than double Channel 0's request volume.
(Likely cause: our workload's strided/random phases have some structure --
e.g. an 8KiB stride in phase 2, and the low bits of our xorshift PRNG in
phase 3 -- that correlates with the single LSB channel-select bit rather
than being perfectly uniform. Not investigated further here, but it means
the 2-channel result is not the theoretical best case for this trace.)

--- Task 1 (1 channel) vs Task 4 (2 channels) ---
                              Task 1 (1ch)        Task 4 (2ch)     Change
memory_cycles                 16,700,347          8,641,291        1.933x speedup
avg read latency (cycles)     811.36              780.01           only 3.9% reduction

--- Key finding: throughput improved a lot; per-request latency barely moved ---
This is the "latency barely improves" case the assignment describes.
Doubling channels nearly DOUBLES total throughput (memory_cycles almost
halved, 1.933x), because there are now twice as many independent banks in
total across which FRFCFS can find ready work, and twice as many requests
can be in flight to DRAM concurrently. But the average LATENCY of any
individual completed request barely changes (811 -> 780 cycles, -3.9%),
because per-request latency is bounded by the intrinsic DRAM protocol
timing of whichever single bank that request lands in -- adding more
channels adds more banks working in parallel, but does not shrink the
fixed activate/precharge/column-access sequence any single request must
individually wait through.

--- Identifying the binding timing parameter ---
Method: (1) confirm the "improves throughput, not latency" signature above
(rules out channel/bandwidth contention as the per-request bottleneck --
if that were binding, more channels would directly cut per-request
latency, not just total completion time); (2) read the actual DDR4_3200AA
timing preset values directly from source
(src/dram/impl/DDR4.cpp, "DDR4_3200AA" row, column header:
  rate nBL nCL nRCD nRP nRAS nRC nWR nRTP nCWL nCCDS nCCDL ... tCK_ps):

  DDR4_3200AA = {3200, 4, 22, 22, 22, 52, 74, 24, 12, 16, 4, 8, ...}
    -> nRCD (tRCD) = 22 cycles
       nRP  (tRP)  = 22 cycles
       nRAS (tRAS) = 52 cycles
       nCCDS/nCCDL (tCCD, same/different bank group) = 4 / 8 cycles
  (all in DRAM-clock cycles, tCK = 625 ps, consistent with a real
   DDR4-3200 CL=22 part)

  tRAS (52 cycles) is by far the largest of the four: 2.36x tRCD, 2.36x
  tRP, and 6.5x-13x tCCD.

(3) cross-reference with the row-buffer composition of this trace: writes
outnumber reads more than 3:1 (1,717,626 vs 524,448) and have very poor
locality (~29% row-buffer hit rate from Tasks 1-3), so roughly 70% of all
writes trigger a full PRECHARGE-then-ACTIVATE sequence on their bank. Any
such sequence must first satisfy tRAS on the row currently open in that
bank (a row cannot be precharged until it has been open for at least
tRAS) before the PRE can even issue, and only then can the new row's
tRCD begin. Because tRAS gates the START of the PRE/ACT chain and is
2.4x longer than either tRCD or tRP individually, it is the dominant
single term in the latency of every row-conflict access -- which is the
majority case for the write-dominated portion of this trace.

Conclusion: tRAS is the binding parameter. It cannot be hidden by adding
channels because it is a strictly PER-BANK constraint -- every bank in
every channel must independently satisfy its own tRAS before that bank's
row can be closed and reopened, regardless of how many other channels or
banks exist elsewhere in the system. Doubling channel count adds more
banks total (letting more row-conflict events proceed concurrently across
different banks, which is exactly why total memory_cycles nearly halved),
but it cannot shrink the tRAS floor any individual bank's own row-conflict
sequence must wait through, which is exactly why the average latency of
any single completed request barely improved.
